How the Printing Press Changed Knowledge

The printing press did more than make books faster. It changed who could access, share, and challenge knowledge.

THE PROBLEM

Before information could spread, someone had to copy it.

Before the printing press, books were usually copied by hand. This made information slow, expensive, and difficult to reproduce.

05

THE SHIFT

Who gets the information?

The printing press did not instantly make everyone literate. But it helped change the conditions under which information could be reproduced and distributed.

Information was difficult to reproduce.

Expensive Slow Limited copies

Access to written knowledge was limited by the time, money, and labor required to reproduce it.

The problem changed.

We went from struggling to access information to struggling to decide what information to trust.

More information can also mean more responsibility.

When information becomes easier to reproduce and spread, humans have to make decisions about what deserves to be believed.

THEN → NOW

Every technology changes our relationship with knowledge.

The printing press was not the end of this transformation. It was one step in a much longer history of changing how humans create and share information.

NOW THINK ABOUT IT

What happens when information is no longer scarce?

The printing press made information easier to reproduce. The Internet made information easier to distribute. AI is beginning to make information easier to generate.

THE HUMAN QUESTION

Technology changes what humans can do. But it can also change how humans experience the world.

The printing press changed how humans experienced knowledge. Today, technologies like the Internet and AI are changing that relationship again.

If technology keeps changing what humans can create, share, and understand, what makes the experience uniquely human?
